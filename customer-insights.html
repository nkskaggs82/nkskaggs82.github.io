Customer Insights:
the whole customer in one click

When a call comes in, the agent clicks one button on the case and gets a snapshot of the customer in seconds: their package and price, current and available offers, recent cases and why they're calling today. Then it tells them what to do next. Loyalty calls get the best offer and CARES script lines. Tech calls get a repeat-issue check, the right Knowledge article and a loyalty warning.

The problem

Every second counts when the customer is already upset

Anyone who's worked in a call center knows the first minute sets the tone. The customer is often frustrated before you even say hello: their bill went up, their service is down, or they've already been transferred twice. The account pops up on screen, but the agent still has to click through offer history and past cases to get a quick overview of the customer and their history, all while the customer waits.

I've seen it from both sides. The Salesforce 360 console I work in is going through a lot of change right now as all of our source systems are brought into it, and a one-click summary like this isn't available to us yet. Agentforce could handle it if it were configured for it, but today it's used to summarize case comments, review billing, etc. I wanted to show what's possible: one button that gives the agent everything they need, in seconds. That's faster than typing a question or pasting a prompt mid-call.

Too many screens

Package, price, offers and past cases live in different places, so the first minute of the call goes to searching.

Offer guesswork

Agents have to work out which offers a customer still qualifies for and do the savings math themselves, live on the call.

Missed save signals

A tech call from someone who threatened to cancel last month looks like any other tech call unless the agent happens to dig.

The solution

One button, one popup, nothing to save

I added a Customer Insights button to both the Loyalty and Technical Support case pages. It opens a popup with the snapshot and call guidance, and closes when the agent clicks Finish. Nothing is written to the case, so the record stays clean and every run uses live data.

Every call: the snapshot

  • Customer, product and package
  • Tenure and what they pay today
  • Current offer, plus offers that already ended
  • Offers still available to give
  • Why they're calling today
  • Up to 3 recent cases with what happened

Then guidance by call type

  • BEST OFFERLoyalty: the smallest available offer, with new price and monthly and yearly savings, plus a backup
  • LEAD OPTIONLoyalty with no offers left: the closest cheaper package
  • CARES script lines: Connect, Be Accountable, Research, Be the Expert, Summarize
  • ARTICLE TO USETech: the Knowledge article that matches the issue
  • REPEAT ISSUEand LOYALTY WATCH flags when they apply

Data model

Two custom objects and a few Account fields

I kept the model small so it's easy for the AI to read and easy to demo. DTV Package holds the four packages with regular price and new-customer discount. Account Offer is a child of Account with one record per offer, and its status says whether it's on the bill now, can still be offered, or already ended. Account got Customer Since and a Current Package lookup.

Under the hood

The flows and the prompt

Customer Insights Data: the flow that makes it work

This is the engine behind the popup. It runs inside the prompt every time the button is clicked, gathers the customer's data, and does the package pricing math before the AI ever sees it.

  • Offers: pulls every offer on the account with its status, so offers that already ended are never offered again.
  • Past cases: pulls the customer's recent cases (not today's), so the AI can catch repeat issues and past cancel threats.
  • Packages: checks every cheaper package, prices it with that package's new-customer discount, compares it to what the customer pays now, and only keeps packages that actually lower the bill.
  • Lead option: flags the closest step down, with monthly and yearly savings already calculated.
  • Knowledge: pulls published article titles and summaries, so the AI can match the right article to tech issues.
Key design decision

The flow does the logic. The AI writes the words.

In early testing the model got numbers wrong. It reordered packages, did the savings math incorrectly, and picked a different "best" option on each run. So I moved the package logic into Flow: sorting packages, pricing each one with its new-customer discount, calculating monthly and yearly savings, and flagging the lead option. The AI turns that verified data into readable guidance. The only math it still does is simple offer subtraction (current price minus the offer), which I checked on every demo case. I also added guardrails in the prompt: it's told not to invent offers, conditions or channel lineups, and to only point to a Knowledge article that matches the issue.

What I learned

  • Ground the prompt in a flow, not just merge fields, when you need lists, sorting or math.
  • The more a number matters, the less of the math the AI should be doing.
  • Format in Flow so the output reads the same every time.
  • Old outcomes in case descriptions mislead the model, so clean demo data matters.

Next phase
